# THE JOPETNAH FOUNDATION (TJF)

# CYBERSECURITY AND DIGITAL PROTECTION FRAMEWORK

---

# PREAMBLE

The Jopetnah Foundation recognizes cybersecurity as a strategic institutional priority.

Digital systems support governance, programme delivery, financial management, partnerships, research and communication. Protecting these systems is essential to safeguarding beneficiaries, employees, partners, donors and institutional trust.

This Framework establishes enterprise-wide cybersecurity governance and resilience.

---

# ARTICLE I

# PURPOSE

This Framework shall:

- Protect digital assets.
- Reduce cyber risks.
- Ensure business continuity.
- Strengthen cyber resilience.
- Support regulatory compliance.
- Promote a culture of cybersecurity.

---

# ARTICLE II

# CYBERSECURITY VISION

## Vision Statement

> "To establish a resilient, secure and trusted digital environment that enables The Jopetnah Foundation to fulfil its mission with confidence."

---

# ARTICLE III

# CYBERSECURITY PRINCIPLES

Cybersecurity shall be guided by:

- Security by Design.
- Zero Trust.
- Least Privilege.
- Defense in Depth.
- Continuous Monitoring.
- Accountability.
- Continuous Improvement.

---

---

# ARTICLE V

# INFORMATION SECURITY OBJECTIVES

The Foundation shall protect:

- Confidentiality.
- Integrity.
- Availability.
- Authenticity.
- Accountability.
- Non-repudiation.

---

# ARTICLE VI

# ZERO TRUST SECURITY MODEL

TJF shall implement a Zero Trust approach based on:

- Verify every user.
- Verify every device.
- Verify every application.
- Verify every connection.
- Continuously assess risk.
- Grant only minimum required access.

Trust shall never be assumed solely because a user or device is inside the network.

---

# ARTICLE VII

# IDENTITY AND ACCESS MANAGEMENT

Identity management shall include:

- Multi-factor authentication.
- Strong password standards.
- Single Sign-On where appropriate.
- Role-based access control.
- Periodic access reviews.
- Immediate removal of unnecessary privileges.

Privileged accounts shall receive enhanced monitoring.

---

# ARTICLE VIII

# ASSET MANAGEMENT

The Foundation shall maintain inventories of:

- Hardware.
- Software.
- Cloud resources.
- Mobile devices.
- Databases.
- Network infrastructure.
- Digital certificates.

Each asset shall have an assigned owner.

---

# ARTICLE IX

# NETWORK SECURITY

Network protection shall include:

- Firewalls.
- Secure network segmentation.
- Intrusion detection and prevention.
- Virtual Private Networks (VPNs) for approved remote access.
- Secure wireless networking.
- Continuous monitoring.

---

# ARTICLE X

# ENDPOINT SECURITY

All approved devices shall be protected through:

- Endpoint detection and response (EDR).
- Anti-malware protection.
- Encryption.
- Automatic security updates.
- Device management.
- Secure configuration standards.

---

# ARTICLE XI

# CLOUD SECURITY

Cloud services shall comply with approved security standards, including:

- Encryption of data in transit and at rest.
- Identity and access controls.
- Backup and recovery capabilities.
- Security logging.
- Vendor security assessments.
- Continuous compliance monitoring.

---

# ARTICLE XII

# DATA PROTECTION

Sensitive information shall be protected through:

- Data classification.
- Encryption.
- Secure transfer mechanisms.
- Backup procedures.
- Data loss prevention controls.
- Secure disposal.

The Framework shall operate alongside the TJF Data Governance Framework.

---

# ARTICLE XIII

# CYBER INCIDENT RESPONSE

Cybersecurity incidents shall follow the lifecycle:

```
Preparation

↓

Detection

↓

Containment

↓

Investigation

↓

Eradication

↓

Recovery

↓

Lessons Learned
```

All significant incidents shall be documented and reviewed.

---

# ARTICLE XIV

# BUSINESS CONTINUITY AND DISASTER RECOVERY

The Foundation shall maintain:

- Business Continuity Plans.
- Disaster Recovery Plans.
- Regular backup schedules.
- Recovery testing.
- Alternate communication procedures.

Critical systems shall have defined recovery objectives appropriate to their business importance.

---

# ARTICLE XV

# THIRD-PARTY CYBER RISK MANAGEMENT

Before engaging vendors or partners that access TJF systems or data, assessments shall consider:

- Security maturity.
- Compliance.
- Privacy practices.
- Incident response capability.
- Business continuity.
- Contractual security obligations.

---

# ARTICLE XVI

# SECURITY AWARENESS

Every employee shall receive cybersecurity training covering:

- Phishing awareness.
- Password security.
- Social engineering.
- Safe internet practices.
- Data protection.
- Incident reporting.
- Secure use of AI and digital tools.

Training shall be refreshed periodically.

---

# ARTICLE XVII

# CYBERSECURITY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS

Performance shall be monitored through:

- Security awareness completion rates.
- Patch compliance.
- Incident response times.
- Vulnerability remediation rates.
- Security audit findings.
- Backup recovery success.
- Multi-factor authentication adoption.
- Third-party security assessment completion.

---

# ARTICLE XVIII

# FUTURE CYBERSECURITY VISION

By 2035–2100, TJF shall maintain a globally recognized cybersecurity programme characterized by:

- Proactive threat management.
- Secure digital innovation.
- Trusted information protection.
- Strong cyber resilience.
- Continuous adaptation to emerging threats.

---

# FINAL DECLARATION

> "Cybersecurity is not solely a technology function; it is a shared institutional responsibility. Every employee, volunteer, partner and leader contributes to protecting the mission, people and future of The Jopetnah Foundation."
